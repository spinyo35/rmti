[00:00-00:13] OVERVIEW

RTMI is a real-time football match intelligence dashboard. It combines a
simulated match feed, live match statistics, personalized commentary, an
event timeline, and two AI-assisted review tools: the Analyst Desk and the
Review Room VAR.

[00:00-00:13]
The recording uses the Live theme, which places the interface over a
stadium-and-pitch background. The match shown is Harare Lions (HAR) against
Bulawayo Eagles (BUL). The demo finishes 1-1.

[00:00-00:53] MAIN DASHBOARD

[00:00-00:13]
The top header identifies the product as RTMI, Real-Time Match Intelligence,
and labels the fixture as a demo match. Status indicators show the browser's
connection to the match service and the AI/Foundry configuration state. In
the recorded session, these indicators show Connected and AI Configured.

[00:00-00:13, then updates during the match]
System Health is the narrow status card below the header. It summarizes the
API, simulator, and Foundry states. During the match, the recording shows API
Healthy, simulator Running, and Foundry Configured. At full time, the
simulator changes to Finished. These are service/configuration indicators;
they are separate from the match result.

[00:13-00:23, then updates during the match]
The score card is directly below System Health. It displays the two team
names and crests, the current score, match clock, and match status. The
recording begins at 0-0 while the match is running, shows Harare take a 1-0
lead, and ends at 1-1 at full time.

[00:18-00:23]
The controls beside the score provide:
- A simulation-speed selector, shown at 120x in the recording.
- Start Match or Pause, depending on match state.
- Demo Run, which launches the repeatable scripted match.
- Reset, which returns the match to its initial state.

[00:31-00:40]
Below the score, the broadcast selector offers four commentary personas:
Casual, Stats Nerd, Kid, and Tactician. The selected persona changes the
commentary voice; it does not change the underlying match events or stats.
The theme selector offers Dark, Light, and Live. Live is selected in this
recording.

[00:53-01:05]
The Analyst Desk and Review Room VAR buttons open full-screen panels. These
are separate tools: the desk interprets match events and stats, while VAR
reviews submitted video frames.

[00:31-00:53] FROM THE BOOTH

[00:31-00:40]
The From the booth card contains the personalized live commentary feed. At
the beginning of the recording it is still warming up; as key events arrive,
commentary cards appear for the selected persona. A card identifies its
content type (for example, Live Call), displays the commentary and time, and
includes a Why This? control, a source label, and a confidence value.

[As match events occur; example commentary visible around 00:31-00:49]
One visible commentary explains that Harare scored through J. Moyo, giving
the Lions a 1-0 lead, and cites a 0.42 xG chance. Another card describes
Harare's early possession and momentum. These are generated commentary
examples tied to the match feed.

[00:40-00:53]
Why This? expands the explanation and evidence IDs associated with an
insight. The matching event can be found in the timeline, and selecting a
receipt highlights it. Source labels distinguish Foundry-generated content
from labeled template/fallback output. The confidence percentage belongs to
that generated insight; it is not the probability of the match result.

[00:23-00:55; continues updating through full time] MATCH TIMELINE

[00:23-00:31]
The Match timeline lists individual events with match time, event type,
player (when available), and team abbreviation. The All and Key Moments
filters control which events are displayed. Key events can include shots,
goals, cards, and momentum swings; routine passes and other events also
appear in All.

[As the match progresses; full-time event visible at approximately 00:55]
The timeline is scrollable and shows the latest events, with a count when
there are more events than fit in the card. In the recording it includes the
Harare goal, the later Bulawayo equalizer, and the full-time event. Event
receipt IDs are visible on key entries and can be connected to the
explanations in the booth and analyst cards.

[Approximately 00:35-00:53] AT A GLANCE

[Approximately 00:35-00:53]
Under the timeline, At a glance condenses the current match into three
quick-read values:
- Shots for each team.
- The current possession leader.
- Which team currently has the stronger pressure/momentum indicator, or
  whether play is balanced.

It is a compact summary of the live data, not a separate source of match
events. A receipt note beneath it reminds viewers that insight explanations
can be traced to events.

[00:53-01:05] ANALYST DESK

[00:53-01:05]
The Analyst Desk opens as a full-screen panel headed “Three voices. One
verdict.” It presents three separate perspectives on a selected key moment:

1. Tactics — discusses the late T. Ncube goal from a wide, advanced
   position, citing coordinates and a prior saved shot as context.
2. Stats — cites the goal's 0.31 xG at 88:57, the 1-1 score, and Harare's

3. Fan — describes the late equalizer as a pivotal moment in a tight match.

[Approximately 00:58-01:05]
Each perspective has its own source label and a Why link for the evidence
behind its statements. The cards are AI-generated interpretations rather
than official match records.

[Approximately 00:58-01:05]
A separate Final Verdict card synthesizes the three takes. In this
recording, it describes Ncube's 88:57 goal as a momentum-shifting moment,
mentions the earlier saved shot and Harare's statistical advantage, and
shows 87% confidence. This is the panel's generated interpretation. The
scoreboard and timeline remain authoritative for the displayed match facts:
the goal makes the score 1-1.

[01:05-01:26] REVIEW ROOM VAR

[01:05-01:11]
The Review Room VAR is a full-screen video-review workspace. Its left side
lets the user select a short video clip or load a random synthetic scenario.
The recorded session first shows an away-attack scenario and later loads the
synthetic away-offside-goal scenario.

[01:05-01:16]
The right side contains the sampled-frame player, current frame number,
previous/next controls, Play Animation, and a strip of frame thumbnails.
The scenario description below the thumbnails explains the intended setup:
a blue away attacker is beyond the second-last defender when the pass is
played, then scores past the gold-shirted home goalkeeper.

[01:11-01:21]
The Review Mode selector offers Referee and Analyst. Send for Review submits
the sampled frames and selected mode. A completed result displays the
decision, source, written explanation, evidence frames, linked event
receipts, and confidence.

[Approximately 01:16-01:26]
For the offside-goal sample shown in this recording, the result is No Action,
with Foundry as the displayed source and 40% confidence. Its explanation
says the pictures do not make the precise moment of the pass and the
attacker's position relative to the second-last defender clear enough to
determine offside or a foul. The result lists frames 1 through 8 and event
receipt evt_0194. It does not confirm the goal as offside.

[01:16-01:26]
The Review Room explicitly notes that this is AI-assisted review and that a
human referee makes the final call. The displayed synthetic scenario is a
test illustration, not real broadcast footage.
